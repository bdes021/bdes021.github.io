## Professional Self-Assessment

Completing the Computer Science program has helped me develop a much broader understanding of what it means to create a successful computing solution. When I began the program, much of my focus was simply on learning programming languages and getting applications to work correctly. As I progressed through more advanced courses, I learned that software development involves much more than writing code. A good solution also needs to be organized, efficient, secure, maintainable, and designed around the needs of the people who will use it.

Developing my ePortfolio gave me an opportunity to bring these skills together and look back at projects I completed earlier in the program. By improving those projects with the knowledge I have gained since creating them, I was able to clearly see my growth as a computer science student and better understand the type of developer I want to become.

One of the most important skills I developed throughout the program was learning how to communicate technical information to different audiences. Computer scientists do not work only with other programmers. They also need to communicate with managers, customers, users, and other stakeholders who may have different levels of technical knowledge. Throughout my coursework, I practiced explaining design decisions, documenting code, creating technical reports, and presenting projects in a way that others could understand.

My coursework also strengthened my ability to use data structures and algorithms to solve problems efficiently. Earlier programming courses introduced concepts such as searching, sorting, and selecting appropriate data structures, while later projects helped me understand why those decisions matter in real applications. I learned to consider performance, memory usage, and the tradeoffs involved when selecting a solution.

Software engineering has been another major area of growth throughout the program. I have worked with several programming languages and environments, including Java, Python, C++, SQL, Android development, and web-based applications. These experiences taught me that the structure of an application becomes increasingly important as a project becomes larger. Learning concepts such as object-oriented programming, separation of responsibilities, design patterns, testing, and modular development changed the way I approach software.

Database development has also become an important part of my skill set. I have worked with relational and nonrelational databases, including SQLite and MongoDB. I learned how to perform CRUD operations, design database structures, maintain relationships between data, and improve query performance. Understanding indexes, foreign keys, parameterized queries, projections, and validation has helped me create database solutions that are more reliable and efficient.

Security is another area that significantly changed how I think about software development. Through secure coding coursework, I worked with vulnerabilities such as buffer overflows, integer overflow and underflow, SQL injection, and improper exception handling. I learned that security should be considered throughout the development process rather than added only after an application is complete.

Creating this ePortfolio gave me an opportunity to combine these different areas of computer science into a single representation of my skills. The artifacts below demonstrate my growth across software engineering, algorithms and data structures, database development, and security.
